# Do not write shorter prompts to save money

The most common instinct, and the least effective thing you could act on.

**In a real session's logs, everything the human actually typed came to about a
hundredth of a percent of the bill.** Prompt length is a rounding error. You
could halve every message you write and not be able to find the difference.

**Vague prompts do cost you — but not through their length.** They cost you
through the file reads, the wrong turns and the rework they trigger, and every
one of those lands in your context and gets resent for the rest of the session.

So the incentive runs the other way from the folklore. A longer prompt that names
the file, states the constraint and rules out the approach you already tried is
cheaper than the terse one that sends your agent looking.

Write the prompt that gets it right first time. That is the token optimisation.
